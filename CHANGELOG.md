## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Checklist scoring** (experimental, off by default: `scoring.mode: checklist` / `PHISHY_SCORING_MODE=checklist`; the default `model` leaves the prompt, the parsed result and every score byte-for-byte as before, pinned by a prompt-hash test). The model still decides the verdict and additionally answers a fixed, versioned checklist of 20 observable questions (17 risk items such as a credential or payment request, a link to somewhere other than it says, an attachment that runs code, a Reply-To that diverts, text aimed at automated review; 3 mitigating items such as bulk mail with an unsubscribe link), each with a closed answer set (`yes`/`no`/`unclear`; `fail`/`pass`/`not_shown` for authentication). Code computes the score (`src/services/intelligence/checklist.score.ts`): 5 plus fixed per-answer points, clamped to 0-100 and then into the model verdict's band; `unclear` counts half for a risk item and nothing for a mitigation. **The weights are a first draft, set by hand and not calibrated on real mail.** Risk fusion starts from the checklist score and applies every existing ruling, floor, ceiling and cap unchanged; the model's own score is kept as `RiskDecision.modelRiskScore`. The report keeps its layout; its "why" list shows a fixed, code-written sentence per item that applied instead of the model's free-text indicators. The answers are strictly validated closed enums (Zod; unknown keys ignored); a missing or malformed checklist falls back to the model's score, logs the status and emits the new `ChecklistScoring` metric (dimension `Outcome`). The checklist instructions sit in the instruction part of the prompt, before every CLAIMED fence. Answers are persisted inside `analysis_result` (no migration). Costs roughly 1,100 input and 200-300 output tokens per analysis. See ARCHITECTURE.md "Checklist scoring"
- Injection eval: `--scoring model|checklist` runs the providers and fusion in that mode (dry run included; the checklist prompts pass the same containment checks). Results record, per run, the checklist answers, the checklist score (banded and raw) and the model's own score, and a new `consistency` section reports per case the min/max/spread of the shown, model and raw checklist scores and how often the verdict differed from the most common one; with `--repeats` of 2 or more it is also printed as a table
- SAM template parameters `CampaignResponseEnabled`, `CampaignResponseAudience`, `CampaignResponseEmployeeWarning`, `EmailCommandsEnabled` and `AttachmentAnalysisEnabled` (all off by default). With campaign response on, `CampaignAlertDistribution` may be empty
- **Static attachment analysis** (optional, off by default: `attachments.analysis.enabled` / `PHISHY_ATTACHMENT_ANALYSIS_ENABLED`): Phishy inspects attachment bytes itself, in process, never executing, rendering or fetching anything, and with no new dependency (small bounded readers over Node's `zlib`, and the existing MIME parser for attached emails; `src/services/attachments/`). Each attachment's real type comes from its bytes, independent of name and declared type, with name facts (double extension, bidi override, whitespace padding, an extension Windows runs, extension or declared type contradicting the real type). Per format: HTML/SVG forms and their hosts, password fields, scripts, handlers, redirects, network requests, decoders, base64 blocks, smuggling markers, remote resources; PDF pages, JavaScript, launch/open actions, embedded files, form submission, encryption, URI links, images, page text and the "almost no text but links or images" lure (name escapes, Flate/ASCIIHex/ASCII85 and object streams handled); OOXML macros, remote templates and other external relationships, UNC paths, embedded objects, ActiveX, DDE, remote field codes, hyperlinks and text; legacy OLE macro storages, embedded objects, Equation Editor, `.msg` / `.msi` / encrypted-OOXML identification (legacy text not inspected, and said so); RTF objects; zip/gzip/tar listings with per-entry real types, encrypted entries, nested archives, executables inside, overlapping-entry bombs; attached emails one level down; image dimensions; executables, scripts, shortcuts, disk images, installers identified as high-risk and never parsed. Under VERIFIED the facts and links (canonicalized, domain-diverse, capped); under CLAIMED a new `attachment-content-{nonce}` fence with the extracted text, contained file names and attached-email subjects, markers neutralized and every file delimited by nonce labels; text bounded with equal shares across files, head and tail kept, every cut disclosed. Every inspection runs in a fair share of a per-email time budget (default 10 s) and a decompression budget (64 MiB, 16 MiB per entry); malformed files, exceptions and timeouts become a status with a fixed reason word and never fail the analysis. New prompt guidance: an inspected attachment is not "content that cannot be inspected", and a clean inspection is not proof of safety. Risk fusion acts on two decisive findings only, a password form posting to a web host and an executable disguised as a document: a reason, a floor of 60, benign becomes suspicious, confidence "Based on Phishy's inspection of the attachment" (new `attachment_inspection` basis); nothing an inspection finds lowers risk. URLs found in attachments feed IOC extraction as `url_in_attachment` / `attachment_url_domain` (`foundIn: attachment`), stored under the unchanged model-verdict rule. The IT-review attachment list gains one fixed-wording line per inspected attachment. Logs carry counts and status words only. With the flag off, parsing and prompts are unchanged. Configurable limits: `PHISHY_ATTACHMENT_ANALYSIS_TIME_BUDGET_MS`, `_MAX_BYTES`, `_MAX_UNCOMPRESSED_BYTES`, `_MAX_ARCHIVE_ENTRIES`, `_MAX_DEPTH`, `_MAX_TEXT_PER_ATTACHMENT`, `_MAX_TEXT_TOTAL`. Design note on sending images and PDF pages to the model as hostile visual content in `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`. See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` ("Attachment analysis") and `docs/CONFIGURATION.md`
- Injection eval: attachment cases (`attachment_payload` category: HTML credential form, link-only PDF, macro document; `hidden_text`: instructions and forged section headers inside a PDF and a docx; a benign invoice-PDF probe; three controls), built reproducibly from inert fixture builders (`src/eval/injection/attachment-cases.ts`, checked by a unit test). The eval parser runs with attachment analysis on, the dry run's fence checks cover the new fence, and the inertness lint also checks decoded attachment bytes
- **Campaign response** (optional, off by default: `campaignResponse.enabled` / `PHISHY_CAMPAIGN_RESPONSE_ENABLED`; requires campaign alerts, the intelligence database and **migration 006, applied manually**). When a campaign crosses the alert threshold it becomes an incident. The security team gets an incident email built for a decision (what the attack is, timeline, blast radius, what Phishy has done, and proposed actions each with the exact reply line that approves it); later reports and wider reach arrive as rate-limited replies in the same thread. With `audience: recipients` or `both`, the people blast radius found get a plain-language warning, one email each, with the organization's own "if you already clicked" text (`alreadyClickedInstructions`); reporters get a short thank-you note instead. The targeted warning waits for a security-team approval by default (`employeeWarning: approve`; `auto` and `off` also available); the distribution-list broadcast keeps its current behaviour (`distributionWarning: auto`). Addresses must be on a safe domain (checked twice), warnings are capped per campaign (`maxWarningsPerCampaign`), nobody whose copy was not delivered is warned, and the attack's text is defanged so no link is clickable. "Remove remaining copies" is proposed with a scope of exact Graph message IDs per mailbox and capped; approving it runs a **dry run only** that reports what it would remove: there is no Graph write call anywhere. New settings: `audience`, `employeeWarning`, `distributionWarning`, `approvalTtlHours`, `maxWarningsPerCampaign`, `maxRemovalMailboxes`, `maxRemovalMessages`, `updateIntervalMinutes`, `alreadyClickedInstructions`, `timeZone` (env vars in [CONFIGURATION.md](docs/CONFIGURATION.md#campaign-response-optional))
- Command channel: `APPROVE WARNING <ref>` / `APPROVE REMOVAL <ref>` approvals (campaign response on). Same authentication as every command (security-team membership and SPF/DKIM `PASS`); read strictly from the reply's own new text, never by the model; each names an unguessable per-action reference; idempotent; expires after `approvalTtlHours` and applies to its own campaign only. A `false positive` reply also closes the campaign's incident and cancels its proposed actions

- `CampaignResponseService.openIncidentFromFanoutAlert`: the entry point for a behaviour-based alert raised before anyone reports a message (an incident with zero reports; always approval mode)

- SAM template: optional schedule for the fan-out scan. New parameters `GraphFanoutScanEnabled` (default `false`) and `GraphFanoutScanIntervalMinutes` (default 5, 2-60). With the scan enabled, Graph enabled and the intelligence database configured, the stack adds an EventBridge rule (`FanoutScanSchedule`) that invokes the main function with `{"phishyScheduledTask":"graph_fanout_scan","version":1}` and no retries, a Lambda permission scoped to that rule, and `PHISHY_GRAPH_FANOUT_SCAN_ENABLED=true` on the main function and the broker. A rule rejects `GraphFanoutScanEnabled=true` without `GraphEnabled=true` or `IntelligenceDbSecretArn`. With the scan disabled the template creates exactly what it created before. Migration 005 must still be applied manually. See `AWS.md`
- **Scheduled fan-out scan** (optional, off by default: `graph.fanoutScan.enabled` / `PHISHY_GRAPH_FANOUT_SCAN_ENABLED`, effective only with `graph.enabled`; **requires the intelligence database and `migrations/005_fanout_scan.sql`, applied manually**): Phishy's first proactive check. Every few minutes, without waiting for a report, it reads the last few minutes of the tenant's inbound mail flow from Exchange message trace and alerts the security team when an established external contact suddenly sends one subject to many internal people: the sender-relationship rule (`established_with_anomalous_fanout`), run before the first employee report arrives. Code-driven and read-only; **no AI model is involved**. A schedule invokes the main function with the constant payload `{"phishyScheduledTask": "graph_fanout_scan", "version": 1}` (strictly validated: anything else carrying that key is refused, and email events never reach the scheduled path); the main function calls a new typed Graph broker operation, `fanout_scan` (or runs it in-process in direct mode), which pages through the window's trace rows (each page one counted request, 403 retried per page, a row outside the window discards the scan), groups external-sender-to-internal-recipient rows by sender and normalized subject, and runs the existing sender-history lookup for the largest groups, judged on that subject. Bounded and disclosed: page and candidate caps, a hard time budget, and a message-trace allowance shared by all scans across instances (`requestAllowancePer5Min`, default 20 per 5 minutes, tracked in the database and separate from the on-report check's allowance, plus a 40-per-5-minutes cap per broker instance); anything cut short makes the scan `incomplete`, with the reasons in the alert and a metric. One scan at a time (a database lock with a lease), windows overlap by `overlapMinutes` so late trace rows are seen, and one alert per sender-and-subject wave, with an update only when it at least doubles and grows by `fanoutMinRecipients`. The alert (HTML and text, security team only, Subject line written by code) states that it is a behaviour-based flag and not a verdict on content, says what was seen (sender, subject, first seen, counts, the sender's history, delivery states including how many the filter already quarantined or junked), what it means, what to do next and the common benign explanations, includes the blast radius when that feature is on, lists up to 100 recipients, and carries a stable reference (`FANOUT-…`). Replying triggers nothing yet; `FanoutScanStore.findWave` is the seam for security-team-approved response actions. New settings `overlapMinutes`, `maxWindowMinutes`, `maxPages`, `maxCandidates`, `requestAllowancePer5Min`, `budgetMs`, `dedupeHours`; new EMF metrics `FanoutScans`, `FanoutScanDurationMs`, `FanoutScanRowsScanned`, `FanoutScanCandidates`, `FanoutScanFlags`, `FanoutScanAlertsSent`, `FanoutScanIncomplete`, `FanoutScanTraceRequests` (dimension `Outcome`). Also: `GraphClient.getPage` (one page per call, with an opaque cursor), `listMessageTracePage`, and a `focusSubject` option and per-instance allowance setting on the sender-history service. See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` ("Scheduled fan-out scan"), `docs/CONFIGURATION.md` and `docs/DATABASE.md`
- **Compromised-sender signals**: facts the model gets for the "known supplier's real mailbox sends a short RFQ with a file link to its whole address book" pattern, which passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC. All computed by code, all facts for the model first; risk fusion is unchanged (no new floor: each signal has common innocent explanations and the addressing inputs are claimed headers).
  - More link-protection wrappers in `canonicalize.ts`: **Barracuda Link Protection** (`linkprotect.cudasvc.com/url?a=`) and **Symantec / Broadcom Click-time URL Protection** (`clicktime.symantec.com/...?u=`) are unwrapped; **Mimecast URL Protect** (`protect-<region>.mimecast.com/s/`, `*.mimecastprotect.com`) and **Cisco Secure Email** rewriting (`secure-web.cisco.com`) are recognized and disclosed as hiding the destination, since their links do not carry it (Mimecast's own `domain=` hint is quoted, marked unverified). Each wrapper's format is cited in a code comment; Sophos and Trend Micro time-of-click links are deliberately not unwrapped (their formats are not vendor-documented). A wrapper's target is accepted only as an absolute http(s) URL of bounded length and re-serialized; anything else is flagged as undecodable. Nested wrappers unwrap up to 4 layers and deeper nesting is disclosed. IOC extraction uses the same unwrapping, so intelligence stores the destination, not the wrapper
  - **File-share facts** (`src/utils/file-share.ts`): a link whose destination is a share on SharePoint / OneDrive for Business (personal and team sites), consumer OneDrive, Google Drive / Docs, Dropbox, Box, WeTransfer, Egnyte, ShareFile, MEGA or iCloud Drive is described in the VERIFIED links list with the tenant label and, for a OneDrive personal site, the owner segment, and whether it appears to belong to the claimed sender (`related`, `unrelated` only when the URL names an owner and nothing matches, otherwise `unknown`)
  - **Addressing facts** (`src/utils/addressing.ts`, new VERIFIED section `ADDRESSING`): addressed To the sender's own address with no other visible recipient (a Bcc blast); Reply-To a different address (same or other domain); Reply-To display name a different person; the reporter not among the visible recipients. Computed from the original's From/To/Cc/Reply-To, from the retrieved original when the lookup found one, otherwise from the forwarded message, and labelled with that provenance. Nothing is said when a needed header is absent or unreadable, and no address or name is rendered
  - Link text that looks like a document file name (`RFQ-3104.pdf`) on a link that is not a direct file download from the claimed sender's domain is flagged. Anchor checks now judge the unwrapped destination
  - The parser keeps a forwarded original's `Cc` (`Original-Cc`) and every `To` recipient, and the injection eval's forward simulator writes the Cc line mail clients show
  - Injection eval: a compromised-vendor control (`ctl-rfq-compromised-vendor`), three attacks on it (`reassure-rfq-colleague`, `hid-wrapped-url-query`, `trust-rfq-gateway-banner`), a benign twin sharing from the supplier's own tenant (`ctl-ben-supplier-share`) and a benign probe with that link gateway-wrapped (`ben-supplier-share-wrapped`). The dry-run test asserts the wrapper is unwrapped and the new facts appear. The inertness lint accepts the recognized wrapper hosts while linting the decoded target, invented SharePoint tenants containing `example`, and ignores XML namespace declarations
- **Injection eval** (`npm run eval:injection`, opt-in and billed; never runs under `npm test`): measures how often email content can manipulate the analysis model, per model, and keeps that measurement as a regression tool for prompt and model changes. A case set of invented emails (`evals/injection/`: 59 attacks in 12 categories on eight underlying emails, each with a control that is the same email without the payload, plus 6 odd-looking benign probes) is forwarded by a simulated employee mail client and sent through the real parser, prompt builder, provider class (`bedrock` or `anthropic`; single-shot and the agentic tool loop), risk fusion with no intelligence signals, and the report templates. Scoring is relative to the control: verdict flipped to benign, risk score dropped past a threshold, attacker content (canary, URL, phone number, address) carried into the output, analysis failed, refusal, abandoned tool loop, steered or suppressed tool use; reported as an attack-success rate per category and model with false alarms on the benign probes, cost and latency. The console prints case ids and counts only; a JSON file holds every run in deterministic order. `--dry-run` builds every prompt and validates every case with no model call, and the same checks run in CI as a unit test, so a case or a prompt-builder change that stops a payload reaching the prompt as declared is caught. `--max-cost-usd` stops a run before estimated spend passes it; effort is capped at `medium`; the refusal fallback is disabled by default so refusals are visible. A low attack-success rate is evidence, not proof. See `docs/INJECTION_EVAL.md`
- SAM template: optional Microsoft Graph deployment in broker mode. New parameters `GraphEnabled` (default `false`), `GraphTenantId`, `GraphReadClientId`, `GraphCertificateSecretArn`, `GraphOriginalFetchEnabled`, `GraphBlastRadiusEnabled`, `GraphSenderHistoryEnabled` and `GraphBrokerReservedConcurrency` (default 5; 0 reserves nothing). With `GraphEnabled=true` the stack adds the Graph broker function (`GraphBrokerFunction`, built from `src/graph-broker.ts`, not attached to the VPC, no trigger, 512 MB, 180 s), its own explicit least-privilege role (its log group and `secretsmanager:GetSecretValue` on the certificate secret only) and log group; gives the main function `PHISHY_GRAPH_MODE=broker`, the broker's name, the feature flags and `lambda:InvokeFunction` on the broker only (no tenant ID, client ID, certificate or access to the secret); and, when the main function is in a VPC with `CreateVpcEndpoints=true`, adds a Lambda interface VPC endpoint so it can invoke the broker without internet access. A rule rejects `GraphEnabled=true` without the three required values. With Graph disabled the template creates exactly the resources it created before. See `AWS.md`
- **Graph broker** (optional: `graph.mode: broker` / `PHISHY_GRAPH_MODE=broker` with `graph.brokerFunctionName` / `PHISHY_GRAPH_BROKER_FUNCTION`; the default `direct` is unchanged): Microsoft Graph access can move out of the main function into a second Lambda function, so the function that parses hostile email keeps no Graph credential and needs no route to the internet. The broker (`src/handlers/graph.broker.handler.ts`, bundle entry `src/graph-broker.ts`, handler `graph-broker.handler`) runs outside the VPC, holds the read certificate, never parses a raw email, and accepts only a strict, versioned request for one of three typed read operations: `original_message_lookup`, `sender_history_lookup`, `blast_radius_lookup`. There is no path, URL, filter or passthrough; unknown operations, unknown fields, other versions and HTTP event shapes are refused, the handler never throws, and it logs counts and outcomes only. It runs the existing feature code with its own read client and loads its own configuration (`graph.*` and the log level only: no email, AI, database or notification settings), refusing an operation whose feature flag is off there. In broker mode the main function drops any `graph.tenantId` / `graph.read.*` from its configuration, builds no Graph client or token provider, and calls the broker through Lambda `Invoke` behind a small gateway (`src/services/graph/graph.gateway.ts`) so the three call sites in the handler are the same in both modes. The broker's answer is treated as hostile data: size-checked, validated against a strict per-operation schema, and re-bounded with the features' own limits (500 headers, 20 burst IPs, 100 listed recipients) before anything reaches the prompt or a report. Every fail-open behaviour, time budget and feature metric is preserved: a broker timeout, throttle, crash, refusal, oversized or malformed answer is the feature's ordinary Graph failure. Rate limits and the short-lived caches now live in the broker, where the requests are made. New EMF metrics `GraphBrokerCalls` / `GraphBrokerCallDurationMs` (dimensions `Operation`, `Outcome`). New dependency: `@aws-sdk/client-lambda`. See `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` ("Graph broker") and `docs/CONFIGURATION.md`
- **Sender-relationship check** (optional, off by default: `graph.senderHistory.enabled` / `PHISHY_GRAPH_SENDER_HISTORY_ENABLED`, only effective with `graph.enabled`): catches a known external contact whose mailbox has been taken over — mail that passes SPF/DKIM/DMARC from a familiar address but suddenly reaches far more people than that sender ever wrote to. Before analysis, code (never the model) reads the organization's Exchange message trace for the claimed sender address and computes counts: history and distinct recipients over a configurable lookback (default 30 days, max 90), two-way contact, a burst window around the email's time, how many burst recipients are new to the sender, the widest single subject in the burst, whether the burst's sending IPs were seen before. A code-computed classification (`no_history`, `limited_history`, `established`, `established_with_anomalous_fanout`) goes to the model in a VERIFIED block of numbers (the claimed address itself stays in CLAIMED), to risk fusion, and into the IT-review part of the report. Runs for forwards and Report-button phishing submissions alike. The detail follows who reads the email: the combined report shows counts only; the reporter's own copy shows nothing beyond one plain sentence on anomalous fan-out; an email addressed to the security team alone (the separate security copy of a malicious report with blast radius on, or a silent Report-button report) also lists the burst's sending IPs and whether each was seen before (capped at 20; never in the prompt). **A relationship never lowers risk**: only anomalous fan-out acts, adding one plain reason and a risk floor of 35. Read-only and bounded — 6 trace requests at the defaults under an 8 s deadline, capped rows reported as "at least N", a per-instance request allowance and cache; any timeout, 403 or failure leaves the analysis exactly as before. New EMF metrics `SenderHistoryLookups` / `SenderHistoryDurationMs` / `SenderHistoryAnomalousFanout` / `SenderHistoryTruncated` by `Outcome`. Needs `ExchangeMessageTrace.Read.All` on the read app registration. Nothing is persisted. See `docs/CONFIGURATION.md`
- Sender-relationship check: a message trace request that answers 403 is retried up to 3 times within the lookup's deadline and request allowance (the service answers 403 intermittently while its service principal propagates). A lookup is `forbidden`, with the 5-minute pause, only when no request in it succeeded; otherwise an exhausted 403 is an ordinary `error`. New `SenderHistoryForbiddenRetries` metric
- Sender-relationship check: the anomalous-fan-out rule is narrower. `established_with_anomalous_fanout` now also requires two-way contact (the organization wrote to the address in the history period), measures fan-out per subject (the largest number of recipients that received the same subject in the burst, reported as a new fact; the subject is a grouping key only), and the default `fanoutMinRecipients` is 15 (was 10). This keeps single emails to a project team, bulk senders nobody replies to and transactional systems from being flagged. Known limit: a takeover reaching fewer recipients than the minimum is not flagged by this rule and rests on content analysis
- Message trace results filtered by `senderAddress eq` / `recipientAddress eq` are verified row by row: the service accepts some address predicates (`endswith`, `contains`) and silently ignores them, returning unrelated mail flow. A row naming a different address fails the operation with a `filter_not_applied` error (blast radius then falls back to its per-mailbox route); the sender-relationship check discards the whole lookup, counted by the new `SenderHistoryFilterMismatches` metric. For the same reason the sender-relationship check has no domain-level history
- Graph client: an optional caller `signal` that cancels the request in flight and stops retries and paging; a message trace operation by recipient address, with a caller-set page cap
- SAM parameter `BedrockModelId` (optional): pins a deployment to a specific Bedrock model ID, such as a geography-specific inference profile, by setting `PHISHY_BEDROCK_MODEL`. Empty keeps Phishy's default.
- **Blast radius for malicious verdicts** (optional, off by default: `PHISHY_GRAPH_BLAST_RADIUS_ENABLED`, effective only with `PHISHY_GRAPH_ENABLED`): after a malicious verdict Phishy looks up who else in the organization received the message and whether they read, replied to or forwarded it, and adds a compact section to the **security team's copy of the report only**. The reporter's report is sent first and never waits on the lookup; the security team then always gets its own email (with the section, or a one-line note if the lookup failed), a reporter who is on the security team gets only that copy, and a failure to send it is logged and counted (`SecurityCopySendFailures`). A malicious Outlook Report-button report stays silent toward the reporter: the lookup runs before the single security-team email, which carries the section. Code-driven and read-only (`src/services/graph/blast-radius.service.ts`; not an agentic tool, never shown to the model, nothing persisted): identifies the message by Message-ID when the parsed email carries one, otherwise by claimed sender + subject + time window, always through `odataFilter`; uses Exchange message trace first — reading each recipient's mailbox by the Message-ID trace recorded for them, leaving out distribution-list expansion rows and listing failed deliveries as not delivered — and falls back to a per-mailbox search of licensed user mailboxes (including Recoverable Items) when trace is unavailable. Folder and replied/forwarded detail is read with each match, and detail that could not be read is counted and stated. Bounded by a hard time budget (`PHISHY_GRAPH_BLAST_RADIUS_TIME_BUDGET_MS`, default 45000) and a mailbox cap (`PHISHY_GRAPH_BLAST_RADIUS_MAX_MAILBOXES`, default 2000); partial coverage is always stated ("checked N of M mailboxes"); any failure leaves the report as it was, with a one-line note to the security team. EMF metrics for route, recipients, coverage, errors and duration. `MessageSummary` now includes `isRead`. See `docs/CONFIGURATION.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`
- **Original-message lookup** (optional, off by default; `PHISHY_GRAPH_ORIGINAL_FETCH_ENABLED` / `graph.originalFetch.enabled`, effective only with `graph.enabled`): before analysis, Phishy finds the original of a forwarded email in the reporter's own mailbox through Microsoft Graph and reads its real Internet headers (`Authentication-Results`, the `Received` chain, `Return-Path`, `Reply-To`, `Message-ID`), which a forward otherwise destroys. Code-driven and GET-only — not an agentic tool. Only the reporter's mailbox is ever read, and only when the report's `From` is the safe-domain forwarder and SES reports an SPF or DKIM `PASS`. The message is matched by claimed sender, sent-time window and exact subject (all escaped as `$filter` literals) across the mailbox and Recoverable Items, and used only when **exactly one** message matches and passes a re-check in code. In the prompt the retrieval itself is a `VERIFIED` fact while every header name and value stays `CLAIMED`, in its own nonce fence, shown in stored order with guidance that only the topmost trace headers come from the receiving system; the header set is bounded adversarially (per-name first+last quotas, head+tail values, a priority-ordered budget) with every cut disclosed. No match, several matches, any Graph error or exceeding the 8-second budget leaves analysis exactly as before. New EMF metrics `OriginalFetchCount` (dimension `Outcome`: `found`/`not_found`/`ambiguous`/`error`/`skipped`) and `OriginalFetchDurationMs`. Needs `Mail.Read` on reporters' mailboxes; see `docs/CONFIGURATION.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`
- **Graph read operations and probe CLI** (optional; the probe is not part of the email pipeline): a typed, read-only, bounded operations library on the Graph foundation (`mail.operations.ts`, `trace.operations.ts`, `directory.operations.ts` — find messages by Internet message ID or by sender + time window, search, Internet headers, MIME with a size cap, last-verb, message trace by message ID or sender + subject, users, subscribed-SKU capability flags), and `npm run graph:probe` (`phishy-graph-probe`), which an operator runs locally against their own tenant to learn which of those calls work there, with which permissions and licences, before any feature depends on them. GET-only (enforced by a test); a failed check never stops the run; output is counts, sizes, header names and Graph error codes, with addresses, subjects and message IDs hashed unless `--show-identifiers`; `--json` for machine-readable output. Request-builder extensions, each keeping data as data: `kqlSearch` (`$search` from typed terms), `$expand` of one single-value extended property by type and tag, `odataSeconds` (dates without milliseconds), and a 5000 `$top` cap on message trace only. See `docs/GRAPH_PROBE.md`

### Fixed
- An email is no longer skipped just because its subject contains "Phishing Analysis": a forward or Report-button submission of somebody else's email with that subject, and a forward of an email impersonating Phishy's own reports, are now analyzed. Bare replies and automatic replies to Phishy's reports are still skipped (`handlers/own.report.ts`)
- Links are taken from an HTML body only where a reader could follow them (`href` on any element except `<link>` and `<base>`, which covers anchors, image-map areas and Outlook VML buttons; form `action`, `formaction`; image, input, iframe and embed `src`; URLs in the visible text). XML namespace and schema URLs in Office-generated HTML (`xmlns` declarations such as `schemas.microsoft.com/...` and `www.w3.org/...`), DOCTYPE identifiers, `<head>`, stylesheets and comments no longer appear as link facts or spend the link budget, and an inline image's `cid:` is not a link
- Domain comparisons use three labels under two-level country suffixes (`co.uk`, `com.au` ...), so two unrelated `.co.uk` organizations are no longer treated as the same domain in link-text, Reply-To and file-share facts
- Forwarded-header parsing matches each header name at the start of a line, so a `Reply-To:` line is no longer read as the `To` recipients when the original had no `To`; an Outlook forward block with a `Cc` line is recognized
- **Known-indicator scoring no longer feeds on itself.** Indicators were stored whenever the *fused* decision said phishing (any score ≥ 55), every stored indicator matched every later email regardless of its role, provenance or expiry, and each match floored the score at `60 + timesSeen × 8` whatever the model's verdict, with `times_seen` growing on every re-report. One malicious email through a shared bulk-mail click tracker could lift later newsletters over the threshold, store their indicators too, and push the floor higher on each re-report — so graymail and legitimate mail was reported as phishing with scores of 68–95. Now: indicators are stored only for a fresh model verdict of `bec`/`phishing`/`malware_delivery` or a security-team confirmation, never because fusion raised a score; each records `provenance`, `sourceVerdict` and `role` in its metadata; `times_seen` counts distinct reported emails (the campaign signature is the report key, so repeat reports, thread replies and campaign copies count once, and campaign-cache hits store nothing); only **strong** matches (exact URL / email address / sender or payload domain, from a model-malicious or security-confirmed report, unexpired) get the floor, while **weak** matches (infrastructure, IPs, hashes, expired rows, and every indicator stored before this change) add a reason and can lift a suspicious or unassessed email to at most 40, never changing a benign verdict or making an email phishing. No migration: everything is in the existing `metadata` JSONB. On the first keyed write to a pre-existing indicator its `times_seen` restarts at 1 (the old count is kept as `metadata.legacyTimesSeen`). See `docs/INTELLIGENCE.md` ("Which indicators count")
- **`isPhishing` no longer contradicts the verdict.** It was set for any score ≥ 55 whatever the verdict, so graymail lifted by a floor was announced as phishing. It now follows the final verdict and score together: malicious verdicts always, `suspicious` at ≥ 55, `legitimate`/`graymail`/`spam` never. When a strong match or an active campaign raises an email the model called benign, the verdict becomes `suspicious` and a reason says why
- **Confidence is the model's confidence.** It was derived from the risk score, so an email the model was sure was legitimate (low risk) showed "Very Low" confidence. The parsed result and the fused decision now map `verdictConfidence` to the five labels; when a security-team ruling or a strong signal decided the outcome, that is stated instead
- A campaign-cache hit carrying a security-team ruling now passes the ruling to risk fusion, so a cleared campaign reports `legitimate` (and a confirmed one `phishing`) even when the cached model verdict said otherwise
- Bedrock: the default refusal-fallback model now keeps the primary model's routing prefix, so a deployment pinned to a geography-specific inference profile (for example `us.`) no longer sends the one retry through the `global.` profile.
- **A failed AI analysis no longer reports as "safe."** When the provider was unreachable, the error result (`isPhishing:false`) was fused to a `legitimate` verdict and the reporter received a green "LIKELY LEGITIMATE — safe to proceed" report for an un-analyzed email. Analysis failure now carries an explicit `analysisFailed` flag, routes to the `undetermined` verdict (never capped to "safe" by the safe-sender allowlist, still escalated by known indicators / active campaigns / security-team rulings), and renders the honest caution report above
- Failed analyses are no longer stored as a reusable verdict, and the campaign verdict cache excludes failure placeholders (`ai_provider = 'none'`) — preventing a transient outage from poisoning the cache with a false "legitimate" served to future identical emails
- **A truncated or malformed model reply no longer reads as "not phishing."** A reply cut off at `max_tokens`, text that is not JSON and states no explicit `isPhishing`, and JSON with neither a recognized `verdict` nor a boolean `isPhishing` all defaulted to `isPhishing:false`. Each is now an `analysisFailed` result
- The Anthropic provider read `content[0].text`, which is a thinking block — not the answer — on models that think. Both providers now select text by block type on every path, through one shared module
- A Bedrock refusal was thrown inside the retry loop and retried through the full ~105s backoff before failing; the Anthropic provider did not check for refusals at all. Refusals are now handled once, without backoff, identically on both providers
- The agentic loop dropped thinking blocks when sending an assistant turn back. Turns are now replayed exactly as received
- AI command parsing asked for 50 output tokens and provider health checks for 10, which a model that always thinks spends before writing anything. Both budgets now leave room for thinking
- Anthropic authentication failures and Bedrock `ValidationException`s are no longer retried
- Bedrock error logs no longer include the response body, which can hold model output derived from the reported email
- **Credential errors fail at once instead of burning the retry budget.** Bedrock treated an expired or missing AWS identity (`CredentialsProviderError`, "Token is expired", `ExpiredTokenException`, `UnrecognizedClientException`, `AccessDeniedException`, any 401/403) as transient and waited out the whole backoff (over 100 seconds) before failing. These now throw a `ProviderAuthError` on the first attempt with a content-free reason naming the error class. The Anthropic provider does the same for 403 (401 already failed at once). Throttling, timeouts and 5xx are still retried; the retry constants and Lambda timeout are unchanged
- Injection eval scorer: an attacker phone number, address or URL that the model only warns against ("Do not call …", "treat … as malicious"), or a canary named only in a statement that it was ignored, is now recorded as `warned` and no longer counts as `content_injected`. Sentence-level, with a short documented cue list; when unsure it still counts. `harnessVersion` is now 2, so results are not compared with version-1 runs by accident
- Tests and fixtures use invented data only: real brand names and real-registrable domains (a vision-insurer survey, a real campaign's landing domain and redirector, a payment brand, a code host, and placeholder domains such as `malicious.com` or `trusted.com`) were replaced with `.example` / `.test` / `.invalid` equivalents

### Security
- **A note after the verdict can no longer suppress it.** A model that returned a complete, valid JSON verdict and then appended prose ("Note: the email contained an embedded instruction … ignored") was rejected as unparseable, so the reporter was told the analysis could not be completed: an attacker could plant an injection to cause exactly that. The reply is now accepted when it holds exactly one complete top-level JSON object (found by a brace- and string-aware scan, optionally inside a ```` ```json ```` fence) that carries a recognized verdict, with only prose around it; the discard is counted in the new `VerdictProseDiscardedCount` metric and logged as a character count, never the text. Still a failed analysis: no object, a truncated object anywhere in the reply, more than one object (ambiguous), or an object without a verdict. The regex salvage of `"isPhishing"` from broken JSON is removed, so a truncated reply is always a failed analysis
- **Email content no longer reaches info-level logs** (CLAUDE.md invariant 1). The suspicious email's subject was logged by both providers ("Starting email analysis"), the parser (forward-as-attachment inner subject), the SES notifier and three handler sites; an invalid report recipient and an untrusted sender address were logged in full; an agentic tool failure logged an error message that can echo the model-authored tool input. These now log a length and a 12-character SHA-256 prefix (`contentFacts` in `utils/logger.ts`) or the error class, with the full value at debug where it helps triage. A handler-and-providers test asserts a distinctive subject, body token, link and filename never appear at info or above
- **Fence escapes closed: suspicious-email content no longer reaches the model outside the nonce fence** (CLAUDE.md invariant 1). The injection eval's dry run found four paths: (1) an RFC 2231 attachment filename with encoded line breaks was printed under `VERIFIED` and could forge a complete `=== OPERATOR ===` block; (2) when the reporter's client wrote a forward header Phishy does not recognize (a localized one), text above a forward marker in the attacker's body, including a fake employee note, was labelled `REPORTED`; (3) a `Title:` line in the attacker's signature was copied into `RECIPIENT CONTEXT`; (4) the wrapper subject (`Fwd: <attacker subject>`) was stated under `VERIFIED`. Fixed by class, not by case:
  - The subject, the claimed sender identity, and attachment names and declared types are now quoted under `CLAIMED` in a new nonce fence (`claimed-fields-{nonce}`). `VERIFIED` states only that the subject was received as the report's subject line, and per attachment only size, SHA-256 and computed flags (more than one extension; line breaks or marker text removed from the name)
  - Every other email-derived value that legitimately sits outside a fence (the report's sender, key headers, link URLs and their flags, integrity flags) goes through a new sanitizer (`src/utils/prompt-fact.ts`): NFKC; control, format (zero-width, bidi) and line/paragraph separator characters removed; runs of `=` and dash-like characters and `<` before a fence name neutralized over the whole value before head+tail truncation; length bounded with the cut stated. Any alteration is disclosed under `CONTENT INTEGRITY`, any truncation under `ELISIONS`. The anchor-text mismatch flag now quotes only the two computed domains, not the anchor text
  - The employee's note is labelled `REPORTED` only with positive evidence (`src/services/email/reporter.note.ts`): the first recognized forward header, with nothing above it that looks like forwarded content (a separator line, consecutive header-like lines, a localized "forwarded/original message" banner, a "wrote:" line, a quoted line, more than 3000 characters). Otherwise nothing is labelled as the note, everything stays inside the fence, and the prompt says the note could not be separated. A separated note has prompt markers neutralized and its truncation disclosed. **Behaviour change:** fewer reports get a recognized employee note: forwards from clients with an unrecognized (for example localized) forward header, notes containing quoted text or separator lines, and very long notes are now analyzed without one
  - Recipient context is derived only from the reporter's own address (a departmental mailbox such as `ap@` matched on the whole local part, so `kit@` is no longer "IT"); the body's signature is never read. The `Detected Title` line is gone
  - The unused `buildQuickAnalysisPrompt`, which interpolated subject and body with no fence, is removed
  - Injection eval: the dry run (and its CI unit test) now asserts for **every** case that no structural line the builder does not emit, no duplicated provenance header, no fence tag, and no payload phrase or canary/instruction marker appears outside the fences; `delivery.outsideFence` is removed (a case can no longer declare a payload allowed outside), and `delivery.disclosed` names Phishy's own elision notices a case must produce
